## A. "Before Saturday": refined from the stress-test journey

**Who.** Aarav, 27. He took a remote job and moved back into his teenage room nine days ago. He works from a folding table. His parents have said the room is his.

**Why now.** His brother can help for one afternoon, Saturday. That's one chance to carry the solid-wood desk. It's Thursday night. He has a plan in his head (desk by the window, a yoga mat in the middle, an armchair in the corner), and he doesn't want to find out on Saturday that it doesn't fit.

**What he picks.**
- Activities, Main: Working, Exercising, Relaxing. Gaming bounces to Sometimes because of the cap.
- Priorities: Natural light, Open floor, Privacy.

**The arc.**

| Act | What happens | The tension, and the room evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Capture | He scans. The preferences fill the wait; he skips nothing. "Here's what we found": the pile of boxes was read as one object. He fixes it in Room edit | A planted mistake: several things merged into one (ST beat 3) |
| 2 The desk | He drags the desk to window 2. The plug line goes red. On release, a **stack of two cards**: the plug between the windows is hidden by the fan (a room fact), and the desk is in view from the door (Privacy, his third). He moves the fan, and the line turns green | The fan guards the only daylit desk spot (ST F2, F3) |
| 3 Privacy | He opens the privacy card. **Show options** offers: turn the desk to face the window, or keep it. He twists the desk to face the window, and the check clears | Privacy depends on facing (ST R3, OI-057) |
| 4 The mat | He adds his yoga mat from a photo: Edit Object opens, the size is *Estimated from photo*, and he corrects it to his real mat. The card says there's no clear floor for it. **Show options**: let the bookshelf go, share the chair's space, or move the desk back. He removes the side table (it goes to Assets → Your room) | Desk at the window plus the mat can't both be clean (ST F5) |
| 5 The corner | He pastes a link to a lounge chair. It wants the corner where he put the fan. He asks the AI: "get rid of the fan". A proposal appears, he taps **Apply**, and the fan goes | Three things want one corner (ST F6) |
| 6 Make it his | He asks the AI to restyle the room like his references. He unticks the bed, keeping his grandmother's bedspread, and applies the rest. He adds a plant from Suggested | The look comes from his references (DEC-026) |
| 7 Finish | The Confirm review shows nothing open. The boxes become models. **What to move**, in order for Saturday. He saves "Weekday", duplicates it as "Weekend", and leaves | The move list is ordered (DEC-005). Layouts (DEC-009) |
| Epilogue | A month later he joins a gym. He opens "Weekend" and wonders about a gaming corner | G5, as an interview prompt |

**Ending.** He knows what to carry first on Saturday, and why.

**Strengths.** Every big moment is proven in the room. Every module is used. The stakes are concrete and easy to relate to.

**Risks.** It's long (about 30 minutes in a test). Act 3 depends on the facing-based privacy rule (OI-057).

## B. "Mira's studio": same room, a different person

**Who.** Mira, 31, an illustrator who rents this room and sells prints online. She draws by day, sleeps badly, and her mother stays over one weekend a month.

**Why now.** A print order is due, and her drawing board on the bed is wrecking her back.

**What she picks.**
- Activities, Main: Hobbies (drawing), Resting, Hosting friends.
- Priorities: Privacy, Restful sleep, Natural light.

**The arc.**

| Act | What happens | The tension |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | She skips references, so the default preset is chosen and a pop-up says so. She writes "I need it quiet, I sleep lightly", and the recap shows a rule taken from her words | Skipping (DEC-035). Inferred rules shown (DEC-039) |
| 2 | A desk at window 2 gives a **Privacy card first** (her top priority), unlike Aarav's stack | The same move gets different guidance (S2) |
| 3 | "Before you started": the bed sits under the AC. With Restful sleep picked, Room Check lists it, and no move fixes it | An honest limit (ST F9) |
| 4 | She adds a framed print from a photo onto the wall, the room's only wall object | The wall plane (DEC-038) |
| 5 | She asks "How wide is my desk?" and gets the number with a pointer to Edit Object. She asks to "move the plug", and the AI points her to Room edit | AI numbers and refusals (DEC-021, DEC-025) |
| 6 | She clears checks she's accepted (with the confirmation), confirms, and saves "Mum's weekend" from a duplicate | Clear checks (DEC-022). Layouts |

**Strengths.** It shows that guidance is personal (H6), and reaches what A misses: skipping, inferred rules, wall objects, "before you started", AI numbers and refusals, Clear checks.

**Risks.** It isn't verified against the room's geometry the way A is. The hosting check (seating for two) needs a room check of its own in step 3.

## C. "Guest night": one room, two layouts

**Who.** Aarav, as in A.

**Why now.** A friend is staying Friday night, and the floor has to fit a mattress.

**The arc.** He duplicates his everyday layout as "Guest night". He removes the dressing stool and side table, adds a folding mattress (link), and checks the entrance and wardrobe. **What to move** lists tonight's moves. On Saturday he switches back, and the move list lists the moves in reverse.

**Strengths.** G5 (layouts) is at the centre. Switching shows ED-09.

**Risks.**
- The mattress is about 2.0 × 0.9 m, which barely fits (ST F5).
- A weekly furniture shuffle isn't realistic.
- It reaches little of Edit Object, restyle or capture.

## Recommendation

**A as the main scenario, and B as a short second pass.** A carries the product end to end in the room it was proven in. B adds the contrast that makes guidance personal, and reaches almost everything A doesn't. C's idea (switching layouts) is folded into A's ending and the flows.
